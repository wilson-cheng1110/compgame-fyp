# How the flip feels

**Hypothesis:** The flip lifts motivation, sense of learning community, and satisfaction — not just scores. Or it costs more effort for no felt benefit. · **Target venue:** Contemporary Educational Psychology

## Seminal / foundational

- Ryan, R. M., & Deci, E. L. (2000). *Self-determination theory and the facilitation of intrinsic motivation, social development, and well-being*. American Psychologist, 55(1), 68–78. DOI: 10.1037/0003-066X.55.1.68 — foundational SDT statement: autonomy, competence, relatedness drive intrinsic motivation; theoretical grounding for the IMI subscales. **Relation:** method/theory.
- McAuley, E., Duncan, T., & Tammen, V. V. (1989). *Psychometric properties of the Intrinsic Motivation Inventory in a competitive sport setting: A confirmatory factor analysis*. Research Quarterly for Exercise and Sport, 60(1), 48–58. DOI: 10.1080/02701367.1989.10607413 — first confirmatory validation of the IMI's multi-factor structure (interest/enjoyment, perceived competence, effort, pressure/tension, perceived choice). **Relation:** method — validated instrument (IMI).
- Garrison, D. R., Anderson, T., & Archer, W. (2000). *Critical inquiry in a text-based environment: Computer conferencing in higher education*. The Internet and Higher Education, 2(2–3), 87–105. DOI: 10.1016/S1096-7516(00)00016-6 — introduces the Community of Inquiry framework (social, cognitive, teaching presence) as the model of a successful learning community. **Relation:** method/theory — 2800+ citations, the CoI seminal paper.
- Arbaugh, J. B., Cleveland-Innes, M., Diaz, S. R., Garrison, D. R., Ice, P., Richardson, J. C., & Swan, K. P. (2008). *Developing a community of inquiry instrument: Testing a measure of the Community of Inquiry framework using a multi-institutional sample*. The Internet and Higher Education, 11(3–4), 133–136. DOI: 10.1016/j.iheduc.2008.06.003 — 34-item CoI survey, 3-factor solution explaining 63% of variance, α = .91–.95 per subscale. **Relation:** method — validated instrument (CoI survey).
- Keller, J. M. (1987). *Development and use of the ARCS model of instructional design*. Journal of Instructional Development, 10(3), 2–10. DOI: 10.1007/BF02905780 — introduces Attention–Relevance–Confidence–Satisfaction as the first motivational-design model in instructional design; field-tested in two teacher-education programs. **Relation:** method/theory.
- Paas, F. G. W. C. (1992). *Training strategies for attaining transfer of problem-solving skill in statistics: A cognitive-load approach*. Journal of Educational Psychology, 84(4), 429–434. DOI: 10.1037/0022-0663.84.4.429 — introduces the 9-point subjective mental-effort rating scale now standard for measuring perceived cognitive load. **Relation:** method — validated instrument (Paas scale).
- Abeysekera, L., & Dawson, P. (2015). *Motivation and cognitive load in the flipped classroom: Definition, rationale and a call for research*. Higher Education Research & Development, 34(1), 1–14. DOI: 10.1080/07294360.2014.934336 — the field's own theoretical bridge between flip pedagogy, self-determination theory, and cognitive load theory; explicitly frames the flip's motivational upside against its potential extraneous-load cost. **Relation:** gap/framing — closest existing articulation of this paper's own hypothesis, pre-empirical.

## Recent & directly relevant

- van Alten, D. C. D., Phielix, C., Janssen, J., & Kester, L. (2019). *Effects of flipping the classroom on learning outcomes and satisfaction: A meta-analysis*. Educational Research Review, 28, 100281. DOI: 10.1016/j.edurev.2019.05.003 — 114 studies: small positive effect on learning outcomes but **no significant effect on satisfaction** (g = 0.05, p = .73). **Relation:** contradicts — directly undercuts the "felt benefit" half of H2.
- Strelan, P., Osborn, A., & Palmer, E. (2020). *Student satisfaction with courses and instructors in a flipped classroom: A meta-analysis*. Journal of Computer Assisted Learning, 36(3), 378–399. DOI: 10.1111/jcal.12421 — 53 studies, 8,429 students: weak–moderate positive effect on course satisfaction (g = 0.36) and instructor satisfaction (g = 0.40). **Relation:** supports (but weak effect size, and disagrees with van Alten et al. above — genuine split in the literature).
- Zheng, L., Bhagat, K. K., Zhen, Y., & Zhang, X. (2020). *The effectiveness of the flipped classroom on students' learning achievement and learning motivation: A meta-analysis*. Educational Technology & Society, 23(1), 1–15. — 95 studies, 15,386 participants (2013–2019): moderate effect size for both achievement and motivation. **Relation:** supports.
- Deslauriers, L., McCarty, L. S., Miller, K., Callaghan, K., & Kestin, G. (2019). *Measuring actual learning versus feeling of learning in response to being actively engaged in the classroom*. PNAS, 116(39), 19251–19257. DOI: 10.1073/pnas.1821936116 — active learning produced more learning but **lower felt/perceived learning**, attributed to the added cognitive effort making students more aware of their own gaps. **Relation:** contradicts — the sharpest empirical statement of the "effort without felt benefit" branch of H2; must-engage-with for the paper's framing.
- Lee, Y., & Kim, K. J. (2018). *Enhancement of student perceptions of learner-centeredness and community of inquiry in flipped classrooms*. BMC Medical Education, 18, 242. DOI: 10.1186/s12909-018-1347-3 — single-group pre/post using the Arbaugh CoI survey in a flipped course: significant increases in all three presences over time. **Relation:** supports — but note design (no control group) is the same one this paper should avoid repeating; closest prior use of CoI-on-flip.
- Wu, Q., Han, Z., Hao, Z., Chen, J., & Pan, Y. (2025). *Impact of flipped classroom method in physical education on the intrinsic motivation, self-efficacy, and learning satisfaction: A meta-analysis*. PLOS ONE, 20(5), e0324609. DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0324609 — 13 studies: flip significantly improved intrinsic motivation, self-efficacy, and learning satisfaction vs. traditional teaching. **Relation:** supports (domain-specific, PE only).
- de Leng, B., & Pawelka, F. (2020). *The cognitive load of the in-class phase of a flipped classroom course on radiology: Could computer support be of help?* Medical Teacher, 43(3), 1–8. DOI: 10.1080/0142159X.2020.1841890 — n=135, used a Paas-style load questionnaire on the flip's in-class phase: high intrinsic/germane load, low extraneous load — cognitively demanding but productively so, when well-designed. **Relation:** method + nuance — evidence that a well-designed flip need not cost extraneous effort, conditional on design quality.

## Gap this paper fills

Prior work tests flip's effect on motivation, CoI, or satisfaction largely one construct at a time and mostly with single-group pre/post or between-course designs (e.g., Lee & Kim 2018 use CoI alone with no control; the PE meta-analysis pools studies that rarely combine IMI + CoI + ARCS + Paas together). The two leading meta-analyses on satisfaction directly disagree (Strelan et al. 2020 vs. van Alten et al. 2019), and no located study jointly measures the four co-equal affective constructs (motivation, community, satisfaction, cognitive load) within the same within-subjects, per-topic randomized design this project uses. This paper's contribution is measuring all four constructs together, per-topic and within-subjects, so it can adjudicate whether a felt-experience benefit exists net of effort cost — rather than picking one construct and assuming the others follow.

## Scoop / contradiction watch

- **van Alten et al. (2019)** and **Deslauriers et al. (2019)** are the two strongest existing counter-evidence sources: flip/active learning can raise objective learning while satisfaction stays flat or felt-learning drops. This is the null/negative branch of H2 already in the literature — the paper must engage with both, not just cite the positive meta-analyses (Strelan 2020; Zheng 2020; Wu 2025).
- No exact scoop found: no located study runs a within-subjects, per-topic-randomized design measuring IMI + CoI + ARCS-S + Paas together across many topics in one course. Closest analog is Lee & Kim (2018), which uses CoI alone, single-group, no control condition — not a scoop, but the nearest neighbor to cite and differentiate from.
- Also worth a citation-check pass before submission: an IRRODL paper on integrating CoI with flipped classroom pedagogy and self-regulated learning in preservice teacher education (found in search but not independently verified/read here) may be a closer analog and should be pulled and read in full before the related-work section is finalized.
