# Can a small local model run this?

**Hypothesis:** A sub-5-billion-parameter model on local hardware (no cloud) is good enough to act as both a Socratic tutor and a blind short-answer grader in a live study — and where it isn't, the failure modes are characterisable.
**Target venue:** COLM (Conference on Language Modeling); alt EMNLP

## Seminal / foundational

- Zheng, L., Chiang, W.-L., Sheng, Y., et al. (2023). *Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena*. NeurIPS 2023. arXiv:2306.05685 — introduces the LLM-as-judge paradigm; strong models (GPT-4-class) reach ~80% agreement with human experts but exhibit position, verbosity, and self-enhancement bias. **Relation:** method (this is the field's baseline instrument and bias taxonomy) + gap (evaluated only frontier cloud models, not small/local ones — the ceiling this paper's sub-5B grader is being measured against).
- Educational Data Mining Society (2023). *Evaluating Quadratic Weighted Kappa as the Standard Performance Metric for Automated Essay Scoring*. EDM 2023 (long paper). https://educationaldatamining.org/EDM2023/proceedings/2023.EDM-long-papers.9/index.html — examines QWK's limitations (kappa paradox, prevalence sensitivity, diagonal-position effects) as the default LLM-vs-human agreement metric in scoring tasks. **Relation:** method (justifies/critiques the agreement statistic a blind-grading evaluation would report).
- (Survey, 2024). *A Survey of Small Language Models*. arXiv:2410.20011 — foundational, broad landscape survey of sub-10B model architectures, training, and capability trends. **Relation:** foundational background for defining "small" and situating a sub-5B model's expected capability tier.

## Recent & directly relevant

- Subramanian, Elango, Gungor (2025). *Small Language Models (SLMs) Can Still Pack a Punch: A Survey*. arXiv:2501.05465 — synthesises evidence that modern SLMs, given better data/distillation, are competitive on specialised tasks despite sub-7B size. **Relation:** supports (capability-tier plausibility for the hypothesis).
- (2025). *MobileLLM-R1: Exploring the Limits of Sub-Billion Language Model Reasoners with Open Training Recipes*. arXiv:2509.24945 — empirically maps a reasoning capability boundary near the sub-2B/sub-1B tier; sub-400M models produce unreliable outputs on competition-math/coding benchmarks, while purpose-trained sub-billion models can still beat larger general models on narrow tasks. **Relation:** supports + method (concrete boundary data for "where it isn't" characterisation).
- (2025). *Quantization Hurts Reasoning? An Empirical Study on Quantized Reasoning Models*. arXiv:2504.04823 — shows 4-bit quantization causes disproportionate accuracy loss on smaller models (e.g. >60% relative drop on a 0.5B model vs. 2–3% on a 7B model) and that degradation worsens with task complexity. **Relation:** contradicts/qualifies (a *local* deployment typically means quantized weights — this is a direct threat to "good enough locally" that the paper must address or measure).
- (2026). *LLM-as-a-Grader: Practical Insights from Large Language Models for Short-Answer and Report Evaluation*. Information (MDPI), 17(5):505. DOI: 10.3390/info17050505 — reports strong correlation with human graders (up to r=0.98) but only 55% exact-score agreement on quiz-style short answers. **Relation:** supports + gap (direct short-answer grading precedent, but on larger/cloud models).
- (2025). *Can Language Models Grade Algebra Worked Solutions? Evaluating LLM-Based Autograders Against Human Grading*. EDM 2025 (poster/demo). https://educationaldatamining.org/EDM2025/proceedings/2025.EDM.poster-demo-papers.290/index.html — GPT-4.1-mini w/ self-consistency reaches 94.47% agreement with human grading; GPT-4o reaches 91.93%. **Relation:** supports (autograding is viable) + gap (all evaluated models are frontier-scale, not sub-5B local).
- (2026). *When Can We Trust LLM Graders? Calibrating Confidence for Automated Assessment*. arXiv:2603.29559 — self-reported confidence outperforms self-consistency for calibration (avg. ECE 0.166 vs. 0.229); argues reliable confidence estimates are prerequisite for safe automated grading. **Relation:** method (calibration approach directly usable for characterising a small grader's failure modes rather than just its accuracy).
- (2025). *Assessing the Reliability and Validity of Large Language Models for Automated Assessment of Student Essays in Higher Education*. arXiv:2508.02442 (journal companion on ScienceDirect) — reliability/validity framework applied to LLM essay assessment in a real higher-ed setting. **Relation:** method + supports (closest real-deployment analogue for a blind grading pipeline).
- (2026). *Reliability without Validity: A Systematic, Large-Scale Evaluation of LLM-as-a-Judge Models Across Agreement, Consistency, and Bias*. arXiv:2606.19544 — evaluates 21 judge models across agreement/consistency/bias with a common protocol; finds a "consistency–bias paradox" where high test–retest reliability can mask severe position bias. **Relation:** contradicts/qualifies (high self-consistency of a small local judge would NOT by itself establish validity — important caveat for the paper's evaluation design).
- Liu, J., et al. (2024). *SocraticLM: Exploring Socratic Personalized Teaching with Large Language Models*. NeurIPS 2024 (poster). http://staff.ustc.edu.cn/~huangzhy/files/papers/JiayuLiu-NeurIPS2024.pdf — proposes a 5-dimension pedagogical evaluation framework for Socratic LLM tutors; finds general LLMs handle verifiable-solution tasks well but struggle with nuanced pedagogical technique absent fine-tuning. **Relation:** method + gap (evaluation framework reusable for the Socratic-tutor half of this paper; evaluated larger fine-tuned models, not small/local).
- (2025). *Discerning minds or generic tutors? Evaluating instructional guidance capabilities in Socratic LLMs*. arXiv:2508.06583 — evaluates whether LLM "Socratic" tutors give genuine instructional guidance vs. generic scaffolding. **Relation:** contradicts/qualifies + closest scoop candidate (see below).

## Gap this paper fills

The SLM-capability literature (surveys, sub-billion reasoning limits, quantization-degradation studies) and the LLM-as-grader/LLM-as-judge reliability literature are two separate bodies of work, and both are dominated by frontier/cloud models (GPT-4-class) even when the paper's stated concern is small-model reliability. No located paper evaluates a single sub-5B, locally-quantized model performing **both** a live conversational Socratic-tutoring role and an offline blind short-answer-grading role within the same deployed study, nor characterises how failure modes differ between the two roles (dialogue-coherence/pedagogical-nuance failures for tutoring vs. calibration/agreement failures for grading) under real local-hardware constraints. This paper's contribution is that dual-role, small/local, real-deployment characterisation.

## Scoop / contradiction watch

No paper combines the tutor + blind-grader dual role on a small local model in a live study — no direct scooper found. Closest adjacent work: "Discerning minds or generic tutors? Evaluating instructional guidance capabilities in Socratic LLMs" (arXiv:2508.06583, 2025) probes exactly the "where a Socratic LLM tutor secretly degrades to generic scaffolding" question this paper needs for its tutor-side failure analysis, and "Can Language Models Grade Algebra Worked Solutions?" (EDM 2025) already reports strong autograder–human agreement (91–95%) — but both use frontier/larger models, not a sub-5B local one, so the specific small/local claim remains open. Two contradiction risks to address explicitly in the paper: (1) the quantization-degradation literature (arXiv:2504.04823) argues small models degrade disproportionately once quantized for local inference — a live threat to the "good enough locally" half of the hypothesis; (2) the "Reliability without Validity" judge-evaluation paper (arXiv:2606.19544) shows high self-consistency can mask poor validity/bias, so a self-consistency-only evaluation of the local grader would not be sufficient evidence on its own.
