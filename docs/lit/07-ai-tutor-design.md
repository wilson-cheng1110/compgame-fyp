# The AI tutor

**Hypothesis:** A Socratic tutor that withholds the answer and detects understanding helps learning more than a plain answer-bot. Or students just want the answer and a Socratic gate frustrates them. · **Target venue:** AIED / IJAIED

## Seminal / foundational

- Bloom, B.S. (1984). *The 2 Sigma Problem: The Search for Methods of Group Instruction as Effective as One-to-One Tutoring*. Educational Researcher, 13(6). https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/1984-bloom.pdf — 1:1 tutoring raised achievement ~2 SD over classroom instruction in the original studies (later meta-analyses revise this down substantially, see VanLehn below and 1982/2020 meta-analyses reporting ~0.33–0.37 SD). **Relation:** foundational motivation — the "why build a tutor at all" premise; caveat the effect size when citing.

- VanLehn, K. (2011). *The Relative Effectiveness of Human Tutoring, Intelligent Tutoring Systems, and Other Tutoring Systems*. Educational Psychologist, 46(4), 197–221. DOI: 10.1080/00461520.2011.611369 — meta-analysis (28 studies, 1975–2010): human tutoring ES ≈ 0.79 vs. no tutoring; step-based ITS ≈ 0.76 (nearly as effective as human tutors); answer-based/substep-based tutors much weaker (~0.3–0.4). **Relation:** supports — interaction *granularity* (step-based guidance vs. answer-only) is the load-bearing variable, which is exactly the axis Paper 07's hypothesis sits on.

- Chi, M.T.H., Siler, S.A., Jeong, H., Yamauchi, T., & Hausmann, R.G. (2001). *Learning from Human Tutoring*. Cognitive Science, 25(4), 471–533. DOI: 10.1207/s15516709cog2504_1 — when tutors were experimentally suppressed from giving direct explanations/feedback and instead prompted students to construct their own answers, learning did not suffer (and interactive, scaffolded dialogue outperformed didactic telling). **Relation:** supports — direct empirical precedent that withholding direct explanation and prompting construction can match or beat telling.

- Graesser, A.C., Wiemer-Hastings, K., Wiemer-Hastings, P., Kreuz, R., & the Tutoring Research Group (2000/2004). *AutoTutor: An Intelligent Tutoring System With Mixed-Initiative Dialogue* / *AutoTutor: A tutor with dialogue in natural language*, Behavior Research Methods. https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/BF03195563 — natural-language, mixed-initiative dialogue tutor built on a curriculum script of hard questions; reports learning gains ≈ 0.8 sigma over reading-only baselines. **Relation:** seminal design pattern (curriculum-script + mixed-initiative dialogue) and supporting evidence for dialogue-based (non-answer-dump) tutoring.

- Aleven, V., McLaren, B.M., Roll, I., & Koedinger, K.R. (2004). *Toward Tutoring Help Seeking: Applying Cognitive Modeling to Meta-Cognitive Skills*. Proceedings of ITS 2004. http://www.cs.cmu.edu/~aleven/Papers/2004/Aleven_ea_ITS2004_HelpSeeking.pdf — with companion: Baker, R.S., Corbett, A.T., & Koedinger, K.R. (2004). *Detecting Student Misuse of Intelligent Tutoring Systems*, ITS 2004 — documents "hint abuse" (clicking through hints to reach the bottom-out answer without engaging), found ~72% of help-seeking actions unproductive, and shows help-before-attempt is negatively correlated with learning. **Relation:** supports — the empirical case for gating/limiting direct answers specifically because free access to answers is misused.

## Recent & directly relevant

- Macina, J., Daheim, N., Chowdhury, S.P., et al. (2023). *MathDial: A Dialogue Tutoring Dataset with Rich Pedagogical Properties Grounded in Math Reasoning Problems*. EMNLP Findings 2023 / arXiv:2305.14536. https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14536 — 3k tutor–student dialogues; models fine-tuned to favor hints/prompting over direct answers produced better tutoring outcomes, whereas un-tuned LLMs "fail at tutoring" by revealing solutions too early. **Relation:** supports + method — dataset/eval design for exactly the Socratic-vs-answer-reveal distinction.

- Sonkar, S., Liu, N., Mallick, D.B., & Baraniuk, R.G. (2023). *CLASS: A Design Framework for Building Intelligent Tutoring Systems Based on Learning Science Principles*. arXiv:2305.13272. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.13272 — dual-dataset training (scaffolding strategies + simulated conversational interaction) for an LLM Socratic tutor (SPOCK, biology). **Relation:** method/design pattern for building a scaffolding-first LLM tutor.

- Google DeepMind (2024/2025). *LearnLM: Improving Gemini for Learning*. arXiv:2412.16429. https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16429 — reframes tutoring as "pedagogical instruction following": models trained via SFT+RLHF on human-tutor transcripts and teacher-authored "golden conversations" to scaffold rather than answer directly; blind pairwise ratings show the tuned model matching or exceeding median expert-human-tutor ratings; very low (0.1%) factual-error rate. **Relation:** supports — closest industrial prior-art for a deliberately answer-withholding, instruction-following LLM tutor; important comparison point, not a scooper of this paper's specific RCT.

- Wang, R., Demszky, D., et al. (2024). *Tutor CoPilot: A Human-AI Approach for Scaling Real-Time Expertise*. EdWorkingPaper ai24-1054. https://edworkingpapers.com/ai24-1054 — RCT, ~700+ tutors / 1,000+ students: real-time AI-generated *expert-like prompts* (not answers) shown to human tutors raised student topic mastery by 4pp overall (9pp for lower-rated tutors); low cost. **Relation:** supports, with caveat — AI augments a human tutor's questioning rather than directly tutoring the student; still evidence that prompting-style (not answer-style) AI assistance improves measured learning outcomes.

- Kestin, G., et al. (2024, published 2025). *AI Tutoring Outperforms In-Class Active Learning: An RCT Introducing a Novel Research-Based Design in an Authentic Educational Setting*. Scientific Reports. DOI: 10.1038/s41598-025-97652-6. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-97652-6 — RCT, N=194 undergraduate physics students: a pedagogically-designed AI tutor produced >2x the learning gains in less time vs. an instructor-led active-learning lecture, with higher reported engagement/motivation. **Relation:** supports — strongest recent RCT that a well-designed AI tutor beats a credible non-AI pedagogy baseline; note the tutor mixes explanation with guided practice rather than being a pure Socratic-withholding design, so it doesn't isolate the withhold-vs-answer variable.

- (2025). *Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning: Evidence from high school mathematics*. PNAS. DOI: 10.1073/pnas.2422633122 (PMC12232635). https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2422633122 — field RCT: unrestricted GPT-4 access (i.e., an answer-giving assistant) raised in-practice performance but *harmed* retained skill once AI access was removed, relative to a version instructed to give hints without the answer. **Relation:** CONTRADICTS the "just give the answer" alternative and directly supports the withhold-the-answer half of Paper 07's hypothesis — closest existing test of almost this exact question, in a different subject/age population. **Must-cite / scoop-watch.**

- (2025). *From Cognitive Relief to Affective Engagement: An Empirical Comparison of AI Chatbots and Instructional Scaffolding in Physics Education*. arXiv:2508.06254. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.06254 — compares a calibrated-hint scaffolded tutor against unrestricted ChatGPT-style assistance; unrestricted assistance reduces stress/raises in-session scores but dissociates from actual learning gains relative to the scaffolded condition (echoed in a companion programming-education study, "Less stress, better scores, same learning," ScienceDirect). **Relation:** supports + contradicts the naive answer-bot-helps assumption; good companion to the PNAS study above.

- (2025). *Socratic wisdom in the age of AI: a comparative study of ChatGPT and human tutors in enhancing critical thinking skills*. Frontiers in Education. DOI: 10.3389/feduc.2025.1528603. https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2025.1528603/full — a Socratic-prompted chatbot condition supported reflection and critical-thinking development significantly better than a standard (direct-answer) chatbot condition. **Relation:** supports — closest head-to-head "Socratic-gated vs. plain chatbot" comparison found, though the outcome measure is critical-thinking/reflection rather than concept-inventory gain.

## Gap this paper fills

Existing RCTs mostly compare an already-designed AI tutor against a no-tutor or lecture/active-learning baseline (Kestin et al.; Tutor CoPilot), or compare unrestricted vs. hint-limited AI assistance on math/programming homework performance and retention (PNAS 2025; the physics/programming scaffolding studies) — none isolate the Socratic-withhold-and-detect-understanding design as a controlled variable within the *same* tutor architecture and content, measured against a validated pre/post concept inventory in a flipped HCI course. Paper 07 also contributes a rare within-subjects, per-topic randomized comparison (Socratic gate vs. plain answer-bot mode of the same RAG pipeline) rather than a between-study or between-tool comparison, and pairs the learning-gain outcome with motivation/satisfaction instruments (IMI/ARCS) to test the "students get frustrated and disengage" half of the hypothesis directly, not just infer it from usage logs.

## Scoop / contradiction watch

Two near-scoopers, both in different domains than HCI concept learning: (1) the PNAS 2025 high-school mathematics RCT and (2) the physics/programming "scaffolding vs. unrestricted chatbot" pair both already show unrestricted answer-giving AI helps short-term performance but hurts retained learning versus hint-only AI — this is close to half of Paper 07's hypothesis and should be cited as the strongest existing evidence, with the gap argument resting on the different population/domain and the added "detects understanding" gating mechanism (not just "withholds the answer"). (3) The Frontiers in Education 2025 study is the closest true "Socratic AI vs. plain AI" head-to-head, but its outcome is critical-thinking/reflection, not a concept-inventory learning-gain measure, leaving that specific comparison open. No paper found that tests an understanding-*detection* gate (vs. a fixed number of withheld hints) as the mechanism, which is this paper's more novel design element.
