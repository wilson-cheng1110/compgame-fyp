# Running a real classroom RCT
**Hypothesis:** A blinded, randomised controlled trial can be run inside a live university course without harming teaching or data integrity — and here is the reproducible recipe. · **Target venue:** ACM Learning @ Scale

## Seminal / foundational

- Kohavi, R., Longbotham, R., Sommerfield, D., & Henne, R. M. (2009). *Controlled experiments on the web: survey and practical guide*. Data Mining and Knowledge Discovery, 18(1), 140–181. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10618-008-0114-1 — Codifies the practical machinery of trustworthy online controlled experiments (unit of randomization, Overall Evaluation Criterion, ramp-up, common pitfalls like carryover effects and Twyman's law). **Relation:** method — the at-scale playbook a platform-embedded classroom trial has to reproduce or adapt down.

- Reich, J. (2015). *Rebooting MOOC research*. Science, 347(6217), 34–35. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1261627 — Argues education platforms have the infrastructure for genuine experiments but default to post-hoc click analytics instead of causal design. **Relation:** gap/method — foundational call for exactly the kind of in-course RCT this paper delivers.

- Hróbjartsson, A., et al. (2012). *Observer bias in randomized clinical trials with binary outcomes: systematic review of trials with both blinded and non-blinded outcome assessors*. BMJ, 344, e1119. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.e1119 — Non-blinded outcome assessors inflate effect estimates by ~29% on average. **Relation:** method — the empirical justification for insisting on blinding wherever a live classroom trial can achieve it (grading, assessment, arm-assignment logic).

- Williams, J. J., Kim, J., Ostrow, K., Xiong, X., Glassman, E., Maldonado, S. G., Li, N., Reich, J., & Heffernan, N. (2015). *Using and designing platforms for in vivo education experiments*. Proceedings of the Second ACM Conference on Learning @ Scale (L@S '15). arXiv:1502.04245 — Direct predecessor at this paper's target venue; lays out how to embed randomized comparisons inside a live course's software without disrupting teaching. **Relation:** method — the recipe this paper extends and should explicitly position against.

- Connolly, P., Keenan, C., & Urbanska, K. (2018). *The trials of evidence-based practice in education: a systematic review of randomised controlled trials in education research 1980–2016*. Educational Research, 60(3), 276–291. https://doi.org/10.1080/00131881.2018.1493353 — 36-year, field-wide audit of how education RCTs are actually designed and reported, surfacing recurring weaknesses (unit of randomization, attrition reporting, blinding, small samples). **Relation:** method/gap — sets the baseline of common failures a methods contribution should visibly fix.

- Grant, S., Mayo-Wilson, E., Montgomery, P., Macdonald, G., Michie, S., Hopewell, S., et al., for the CONSORT-SPI Group. (2018). *CONSORT-SPI 2018 Explanation and Elaboration: guidance for reporting social and psychological intervention trials*. Trials, 19, 406. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13063-018-2735-z — The CONSORT extension for social/psychological (not clinical or eHealth) interventions, including stakeholder-involvement reporting items. **Relation:** method — closest available reporting standard for an in-course educational-technology RCT; the checklist this paper's write-up should satisfy.

## Recent & directly relevant

- Deke, J., & Chiang, H. (2017). *The WWC attrition standard: sensitivity to assumptions and opportunities for refining and adapting to new contexts*. Evaluation Review, 41(2), 130–154. https://doi.org/10.1177/0193841X16670047 — Explains the What Works Clearinghouse attrition-bias model and shows attrition bounds are most sensitive to the chosen "maximum tolerable bias," not the other parameters. **Relation:** method — a quantitative, defensible bound for how much differential attrition a classroom trial can tolerate before bias invalidates results.

- Benbunan-Fich, R. (2017). *The ethics of online research with unsuspecting users: from A/B testing to C/D experimentation*. Research Ethics, 13(3–4), 200–218. https://doi.org/10.1177/1747016116680664 — Identifies consent, deception, and oversight gaps in platform experimentation and proposes concrete governance (consent tooling, an independent advocacy mechanism). **Relation:** method/gap — the ethical seam a "blinded" in-course design has to close, directly relevant to a methods contribution about running an RCT without harming participants.

- Musabirov, I., Reza, M., Song, H., Moore, S., Chen, P., Kumar, H., Li, T., Stamper, J., Bier, N., Rafferty, A., Price, T., Deliu, N., Durand, A., Liut, M., & Williams, J. J. (2025). *Platform-based adaptive experimental research in education: lessons learned from the Digital Learning Challenge*. Proceedings of the 15th International Learning Analytics and Knowledge Conference (LAK '25). https://doi.org/10.1145/3706468.3706471 — Current state-of-the-art account of running randomized/adaptive experiments across multiple live courses at consortium scale, with lessons on what broke operationally. **Relation:** method — the model to benchmark against and improve on for a single-course deployment.

- Baker, R. S., Hutt, S., Mogessie, M., & Valayaputtar, H. (2022). *Research using the MOOC Replication Framework and E-TRIALS*. 2022 IEEE Learning With MOOCS (LWMOOCS), 131–136. https://doi.org/10.1109/LWMOOCS53067.2022.9927934 — Infrastructure paper for running and replicating randomized comparisons across many live course offerings via a shared experimentation layer (E-TRIALS) and replication framework (MORF). **Relation:** method — a replication/scaling angle a single-course recipe should address or explicitly bound as future work.

- Gordillo, A., & López-Fernández, D. (2024). *Are educational escape rooms more effective than traditional lectures for teaching software engineering? A randomized controlled trial*. IEEE Transactions on Education, 67(5), 660–668. https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10571851/ — A real, adequately-powered (n=326) RCT run inside a live software-engineering course comparing two teaching methods, with pre/post knowledge measures. **Relation:** method — closest recent sibling exemplar (same CS/SE-education family, live-course deployment, pre/post design) to benchmark reporting rigor and effect-size framing against.

## Gap this paper fills

The methods literature on running credible experiments *inside* live courses splits into two camps that rarely talk to each other: (1) large-platform/MOOC infrastructure papers (Williams et al. 2015; Baker et al. 2022; Musabirov et al. 2025) that assume dedicated engineering teams, multi-course consortia, or adaptive-allocation tooling far beyond what a single-course deployment can build; and (2) small-N, single-course education RCTs (Gordillo & López-Fernández 2024; the broader population surveyed by Connolly et al. 2018) that rarely report blinding, attrition handling, or a CONSORT-style trial diagram to the standard the industrial A/B-testing literature (Kohavi et al. 2009) takes for granted. This paper's contribution is the reproducible recipe that sits between those camps: a blinded, per-participant randomised design run by a single instructor-researcher inside one live course, with an explicit attrition-bias budget (Deke & Chiang 2017), a CONSORT-SPI-shaped reporting checklist (Grant et al. 2018), and consent/oversight handling calibrated for a captive student population (Benbunan-Fich 2017) — none of which the small-scale classroom RCT literature currently packages together as a "how-to."

## Scoop / contradiction watch

No single prior paper claims the same "solo-researcher, one live course, blinded, attrition-bounded, CONSORT-shaped" recipe end-to-end — the closest partial overlaps are Williams et al. (2015), which is the right shape but assumes a multi-institution platform team, and Gordillo & López-Fernández (2024), which is single-course and real but does not report blinding of graders/assessors or a formal attrition-bias bound. Connolly et al.'s (2018) systematic review is worth citing defensively: it documents that a large share of published education RCTs *already* fail to report exactly the things (randomization unit, blinding, attrition) this paper claims to handle well, so the contribution should be framed as closing that specific, evidenced gap rather than as wholly novel. No paper found that argues *against* running RCTs inside a single live course on methodological grounds beyond the general feasibility/ethics debate already covered above (Benbunan-Fich 2017; the broader "RCTs in education" debate surveyed by Connolly et al. 2018) — none found.
