## 1. Working title and claim

**Title:** Does the game-first advantage travel? A within-student flipped-learning comparison of undergraduate and MSc students in HCI education

**Claim the data supports today (honest):** The FLIP-minus-CONTROL gain gap is directionally larger for undergraduates (<g> gap 0.115) than for MSc students (0.045), but MSc students start at the ceiling (pre about 90/100), so the MSc estimate is low-information and the difference cannot yet be separated from a ceiling or expertise-reversal artefact. No interaction test has been run as a confirmatory analysis.

## 2. Research questions and hypotheses

Matches the 05 stub in `docs/pre-registration/README.md` (candidate confirmatory; MSc inclusion cleared 2026-10-08; stub must be completed and filed before any confirmatory run).
- **RQ1:** Is the flip's gain advantage (H1) of comparable size in the MSc (COMP5517) cohort and the undergraduate (COMP3423) cohort?
- **H05 (stub):** No condition x population interaction on normalised gain <g>. A non-significant interaction supports transfer; a significant negative interaction supports population-specificity. Model = pre-reg 01 mixed model plus condition x population (multi-group logic per Talsma et al. 2023).
- **RQ2 (exploratory, not in stub):** Is any population difference explained by prior knowledge (pre score / ceiling) rather than programme level? Operationalise as condition x pre-score, and condition x population adjusted for pre.
- **RQ3 (exploratory):** Does the difference show on secondary outcomes (retention Form C and application probe, opening about 23 Nov; engagement/telemetry)?
- Note for the stub: because MSc pre is about 90, <g> has a denominator near 10 points and is unstable. Pre-register the raw post-on-pre model (ANCOVA-style) as the primary population contrast, with <g> as secondary, or declare the ceiling rule explicitly. Brief already records <g> overstating (0.52 vs 0.41) in the UG data.

## 4. Review of the field

- Flipped learning has a small-to-moderate positive average effect in higher education (Bredow 2021; van Alten 2019), and active learning more generally helps (Freeman 2014); evidence is overwhelmingly undergraduate.
- Education level as a moderator is unresolved: not reported in van Alten; one thin, health-professions graduate subgroup in Chen 2018 leans null/negative.
- Graduate-only flip research exists but is heterogeneous and low rigor (Oliver & Luther 2020), and none of it was found in computing/HCI education.
- Theory splits: andragogy (Knowles) and SRL work (Broadbent & Poon) suggest PGs may cope well with learn-first; expertise reversal (Kalyuga 2003; Tetzlaff 2025) predicts guided scaffolds help less, or hurt, with higher prior knowledge.
- Andragogy's evidence base is itself weak (Rachal 2002), so neither "PGs differ" nor "PGs do not differ" has solid theoretical ground.
- Contested: whether a UG-vs-PG difference is about programme level or about prior knowledge. Most studies confound them; here pre score lets us partly separate them.
- Newer meta-analyses that test education/class level (Strelan 2020; Gong 2023, via Consensus summaries) report little moderation, which weakens the case that UG and PG differ in flip benefit and leans toward Bredow-style 'flip works broadly'. Chen 2018 is now the outlier.
- Prior knowledge's effect on learning is highly variable (Simonsmeier 2021: prediction interval roughly -.69 to .62 for pretest-gain correlation), and <g> comparisons across cohorts with different preparation are contested (Christman 2024 vs Navarrete 2024), so the UG/MSc contrast should be modelled on pre rather than read from <g> gaps.
- Interactions are hard to detect: median power .18 in typical psychology interactions (Sommet), subgroup tests need 3-4x the studies (Cuijpers). Both the prior 'no level moderation' meta-analytic results and Chen's graduate subgroup are therefore weak evidence either way; within-subject structure helps the condition contrast, but the population contrast stays between-subject.
- Method point: population-as-factor with an interaction test beats post-hoc subgroup splits (Yarkoni 2022; Talsma 2023), but interactions need much more power than main effects.
- Gap this fills: a same-instrument, same-schedule, within-student flip comparison across UG and MSc in HCI, with population as an explicit design factor and prior knowledge measured.

## 5. Argument

1. The flip effect is established mainly in undergraduates (Freeman 2014; Bredow 2021), so transfer to postgraduates is an empirical question, not an assumption (Yarkoni 2022). Rests on cited sources.
2. Existing evidence on the moderator is thin and leans against transfer (Chen 2018; Oliver & Luther 2020). Cited.
3. Theory gives two opposite predictions: expertise reversal (smaller or negative effect) and self-regulation/andragogy (equal or larger). Cited; Rachal warns neither is firmly grounded.
4. Our design holds instruments, schedule, platform, and randomisation constant across cohorts, so population is the only planned difference (although prior knowledge, course, and class timing also differ). Our design (brief; platform facts).
5. Observed: UG gap 0.115, MSc gap 0.045, MSc pre about 90. Interim, exploratory, not tested for interaction.
6. At pre about 90 there is little headroom, so a small MSc gap is what both a ceiling and an expertise-reversal account predict; the two are not separable on one post-test. To come: pre-adjusted models, item-level analysis, and the delayed retention and application outcomes (Nov), which are less ceilinged.
7. Conclusion to be earned: either "no evidence of population difference after accounting for prior knowledge" (transfer holds, with explicit power limits) or "effect shrinks as prior knowledge rises" (expertise-reversal pattern). A claim of population-specificity needs the interaction to survive adjustment for pre score and the retention data. Pending.

## 6. Our evidence so far (interim, exploratory)

- FLIP-CONTROL <g> gap: UG 0.115 vs MSc 0.045. No interaction test, CI, or multiplicity correction reported yet.
- MSc pre about 90/100: ceiling. UG overall ceiling issue also noted: about 73% of post scores = 100.
- Immediate H1 (paper 01), UG-dominated: mixed model FLIP +0.85 to +1.35 points per 100 (p .07-.25); <g> overstates (0.52 vs 0.41). Most of the pre-post gain (about 5.4 of about 7 points) is instrument/retest, not the game (about 1.3). This suggests the MSc gap of 0.045 may be well inside noise.
- Platform parity: same pipeline for MSc; about 74 MSc accounts per the fit map (confirm current analysable N and per-topic MSc volume, which the aggregate monitor view can mask).
- Compliance 99.7%; external help (leaving the page) +5.5 to +7 points within student, a covariate to carry into the population comparison, since MSc and UG may differ in help-seeking.

## 7. Threats and counter-arguments

- **Ceiling/headroom:** MSc pre about 90 compresses any effect. Answer: model post on pre, report headroom-restricted subgroup (pre below some threshold, pre-declared), use retention and application probe; say plainly the test is low-powered.
- **Power for an interaction (supported by Sommet; Cuijpers):** the population contrast is between-subject, so the within-student power gain does not apply to it; condition effect is within, interaction power depends on the MSc N. Report CI and minimum detectable interaction.
- **Power for an interaction (original note):** MSc N is small versus about 340 total; interaction power is much lower than for the main effect. Answer: equivalence/TOST framing or report the interaction CI and what effect sizes are excluded; do not read a non-significant interaction as "transfer holds".
- **Confounding of population with course, instructor, timing, motivation, grading stakes:** Answer: acknowledged; condition randomisation is within student, so it protects the condition contrast, not the population contrast. Population is quasi-experimental.
- **Gain-score vs ANCOVA debate (Kim 2019; Christman 2024):** neither is universally right; pre-register post-on-pre as primary and report the gain-score analysis as sensitivity. Randomised assignment makes both unbiased in principle.
- **<g> artefact:** denominator near 10 at MSc ceiling blows up variance. Answer: primary on raw post-on-pre; <g> secondary.
- **Unproctored, ungraded checks; external help differs by population:** Answer: covariate and sensitivity analysis excluding page-leaving checks.
- **Differential attrition / routing bug:** Gestalt FLIP attrition (fixed 2026-10-08) and navigation bug; check whether MSc was affected differently. Answer: ITT and attrition table by population.
- **Meta-analyses finding no level moderation (Strelan 2020; Gong 2023):** they cut against population-specificity but test course level, not UG vs MSc, and use between-study comparisons. A null interaction here would agree with them; a significant one would be a novel within-study finding. Check their moderator definitions.
- **Reflection may help experts more than the game (Chernikova 2020):** if MSc students gain from the tutor reflection step in both arms, the flip contrast (game position) could shrink for them for reasons unrelated to ceiling. Treat as an exploratory alternative explanation.
- **SRL mechanism is shaky (Sun 2017; Liu 2024):** flip may not change self-regulation and SRL-support results are mixed, so 'MSc students self-regulate better and so benefit more or less' should not be the paper's explanation; frame it as a hypothesis only.
- **Chen 2018 as scoop/contradiction:** Answer: engage directly; it is about 4 studies, health professions, different outcomes; our in-domain, same-instrument design addresses its main weakness.
- **Selective reporting (a candidate confirmatory paper with a favourable-looking gap):** Answer: file the stub (with the revised primary model) before the Nov data are opened; report as pre-registered vs exploratory.
- **Instrument fit:** the MC check may be too easy for MSc; Form C difficulty was already flagged. Answer: use the application probe as the discriminating outcome.
