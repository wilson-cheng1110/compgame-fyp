Built 2026-10-16. Status: feasibility paper. The central number (grader-human kappa) does not exist yet.

## 1. Working title and claim

**Working title:** "Good enough on a laptop-class GPU? A 4B-effective-parameter local model as blind short-answer grader and Socratic tutor in a 340-student HCI course."

**Claim the data supports today (honest):** One locally hosted gemma4:e4b model serves two roles in a live course study with no student text leaving the machine: an interactive tutor (median latency about 2 s) and an offline blind grader whose pipeline (blinding, temperature 0, pinned output budget, human-sample and kappa tooling) is built and has been run. Whether its grades agree with human coders is **not yet known**; the claim is therefore "feasible and characterised on the engineering side", not "valid".

## 2. Research questions and hypotheses (exploratory, no pre-registration)

- RQ1 (grader validity): Agreement between gemma4:e4b grades and two human coders on application short answers, per rubric criterion, reported as Cohen's kappa (unweighted and quadratic-weighted), with percent agreement and a confusion matrix, not kappa alone.
- RQ2 (grader reliability vs validity): Test-retest reproducibility at temperature 0 and under prompt perturbation, reported next to RQ1 so that consistency is never offered as evidence of validity.
- RQ3 (failure modes): Where does the grader fail? Candidates: empty-output cliff when the token cap is too low, verbosity or length bias, over-credit of fluent but wrong answers, rubric-criterion drift, and calibration of the model's own confidence.
- RQ4 (tutor): Does the Socratic tutor stay Socratic (withholds the answer, asks a question) or degrade to generic scaffolding? Operationalised from reflection transcripts coded by humans (shared with paper 03).
- RQ5 (cost): Latency distribution (p50/p95), failure rate (empty or malformed JSON), concurrency behaviour, and what the on-premise design buys on privacy.
- H (directional, stated loosely): kappa vs human is at least "moderate" on the rubric criteria with clear textual anchors and lower on inferential criteria. Stated as an expectation, not a test.

## 4. Review of the field (points)

- LLM-as-judge is established, with a known bias taxonomy (position, verbosity, self-preference) measured mostly on frontier models (Zheng 2023).
- Short-answer autograding by LLMs reaches high correlation with humans, but exact-score agreement is much lower (MDPI 2026: r to 0.98 vs about 55% exact), so the headline statistic chosen largely decides the verdict.
- Agreement metrics are themselves contested (kappa paradox, QWK prevalence and diagonal effects); a sound evaluation reports several (EDM 2023; Landis and Koch bands are convention only).
- Reliability is not validity: a deterministic judge can be consistently biased (2606.19544). Temperature-0 reproducibility is a hygiene property, not a result.
- Small-model capability is improving, but reasoning floors exist and quantization hurts small models most (MobileLLM-R1; 2504.04823). Those results are on reasoning benchmarks; transfer to rubric grading is untested.
- Socratic LLM tutoring has design frameworks (CLASS, SocraticLM) and a live worry that tutors degrade to generic scaffolding (2508.06583).
- Confidence calibration offers a route to characterising failures rather than only counting them (2603.29559).
- Contested: whether on-premise deployment is worth the capability cost. Argument for: student text never leaves the institution (ethics, consent wording). Argument against: cloud models are stronger and cheap.
- Round 2 adds: exact-score agreement is much lower than correlation (dental study: ICC 0.87 but 43% exact); LLMs over-score incorrect answers (Hassanein 2026); rubrics help inconsistently (Bolgova 2025); temperature 0 is not deterministic, even for local 8B models (Nicholson 2026, Coqueret 2026); 4-bit loss on small models is real but task-dependent (Lee 2024, Mekala 2025); local-model privacy feasibility studies for assessment now exist (Rejeleene 2026, Saur 2026).
- **Gap:** no located study evaluates one small local model in both a live tutoring role and an offline blind grading role in the same deployed course, with the failure modes of each role compared under real hardware limits.

## 5. Argument (premise to conclusion)

1. Education research on student free text often cannot send that text to a cloud API (consent scope, institutional rules). Evidence: ethics design of this study (all inference on-box); cite as design fact, not as finding.
2. Therefore a local model is the only option for many such studies, and its adequacy must be measured, not assumed. Evidence: literature gap in section 4.
3. Grading reliability evidence from frontier models does not transfer to a small quantized model. Evidence: 2504.04823, MobileLLM-R1; frontier-only grader studies (EDM 2025, MDPI 2026).
4. A grader pipeline can be made structurally blind and reproducible (arm and participant stripped before prompt, temperature 0, pinned token budget). Evidence: `backend/grade.py` (blinding, seed, `num_predict` 1536 with measured empty-output cliff), `grade_batch.py` (human-sample and kappa tooling). Our data: built and run.
5. Reproducibility is not validity, so validity is tested against two independent human coders on a stratified sample. Evidence: **to come** (coding sheet generated; humans not yet done).
6. Failure modes are characterisable and differ by role (grading: calibration, agreement, token-budget cliffs; tutoring: leaking the answer, generic scaffolding). Evidence: grader cliff documented; tutor answer-request rate (9-10% of reflections ask for the answer) is a stress case; tutor-quality coding **to come**.
7. Cost is acceptable for a classroom: tutor median latency about 2 s on one workstation. Evidence: our telemetry (interim); p95 and concurrency under class load to come.
8. Conclusion (conditional on 5): a sub-5B local model is adequate as a blind grader for criteria where kappa meets a pre-stated bar, inadequate where it does not, and the paper reports which criteria fall where. If kappa is poor, the paper becomes a negative-result characterisation, which still fills the gap.

## 6. Our evidence so far (interim, exploratory)

- Blind offline grading pass has been run on the probe submissions. Counts, kappa and failure-mode tallies: **not yet computed**.
- Tutor latency p50 about 2 s (interim; p95 not recorded in this brief).
- 1,990+ reflection exchanges; 51% reach the tutor's own "insight" flag (the flag is the model's output, so it is not independent evidence of learning; see paper 03).
- 9-10% of reflections ask the tutor for the answer; free chat rare (about 90 questions, a third during checks).
- Engineering facts (code-confirmed in the 2026-09 audit and re-checked 2026-10-16): same default model in both roles (`rag_api.py` and `grade.py`), tutor temperature 0.4 and `num_predict` 512, grader temperature 0 and `num_predict` 1536; grader empty-string cliff below 1024 tokens measured and documented in code. A quick check today found rubrics for all of memory, problem-solving, webers-law, gestalt, stroop, hicks-law and fitts-law, so the earlier "4 of 13 topics only" gap in `data-to-paper-fit.md` is out of date (not all 13 re-checked).
- Offline-by-design: no grade is produced during the student session; students see no machine grades.

## 7. Threats and counter-arguments

- **"Kappa is not computed, so there is no paper."** Correct today. Gate: do not draft results until two human coders finish the stratified sample. If agreement is poor, reframe as a negative result with the failure analysis.
- **"Consistency at temperature 0 proves reliability."** Answer: reported as hygiene only; validity rests on human agreement (2606.19544).
- **"Human coders are not ground truth either."** Answer: report human-human kappa as the ceiling and grader-human relative to it; adjudicate disagreements, report both.
- **"A cloud model would be better."** Answer: probably; a small comparison on the human-coded subset (a cloud arm run only on data cleared for egress, or on synthetic answers) would quantify the privacy-capability trade. Student text must not be sent anywhere; the comparison arm needs ethics clearance or synthetic data. Flag as open.
- **"Quantization degrades small models."** Answer: measure on the human-coded subset by comparing the deployed quantization with a higher-precision run on the same box; evidence in the literature is on reasoning tasks, so transfer is open.
- **"Temperature-0 reruns are identical."** Answer: not guaranteed; log the full software/hardware stack and measure rerun variation directly (Nicholson 2026; Coqueret 2026).
- **"The grader over-credits wrong answers."** Answer: cloud and open-weight graders do (Hassanein 2026); report false-positive rate on human-coded incorrect answers and a length-vs-grade correlation (Saito 2023).
- **"Same model grades and tutors, so failures correlate."** Answer: partly a design feature; test whether tutor-flagged "insight" and grader-graded depth agree, and treat their agreement as circular evidence, not validation.
- **"Rubric authored by the same team that built the model prompt."** Answer: rubric frozen and dated before grading; coders blind to model grade; disclose.
- **"Unproctored, ungraded context lowers answer quality."** Answer: applies to the grader's input distribution; report answer length and null-answer filter rate, and limit claims to this context.
- **"Tutor 'insight' flag is self-graded by the model."** Answer: report it as an engagement signal only; tutor quality comes from human coding.
- **"Single course, single language."** Answer: generalisation claimed only to similar short-answer tasks; mixed English and Chinese input, if present, reported separately.
